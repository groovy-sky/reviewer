This week’s update from Microsoft Devblogs explores how AI coding agents interpret Semantic Versioning labels, revealing variations in their behavior across different models. The study highlights that while some agents may prioritize certain version types differently, the findings do not suggest a universal disregard for versioning practices. The results underscore the nuanced and context-dependent nature of AI decision-making in software update handling.

Detailed list:

* [Do AI coding agents care about SemVer?](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/blog/do-ai-coding-agents-care-about-semver/). - The announcement describes experiments conducted with Microsoft's AI coding agents to determine whether they interpret Semantic Versioning (SemVer) labels—patch, minor, major—as signals for how thoroughly to review software updates. The study involved testing multiple AI models on identical code changes where only the version number differed, with no actual functional changes between versions. Results showed that some agents, like Claude Sonnet 5.5, were more likely to skip verification for patch-labeled updates, while others, such as Claude Opus 5.5, checked all updates regardless of label. The findings indicate that AI agents may use SemVer labels to influence their behavior, though the extent and reliability of this behavior vary by model and context. The study does not claim that SemVer is redundant or that agents ignore it; rather, it highlights that agents may learn from real-world patterns in versioning and documentation.
